The Three Scopes (Decide This Before Any Quote)

“Renovation” means three different projects, and quotes are only comparable within a tier:

  • Refresh: full repaint, minor plumbing/electrical repairs, hardware and fixture swaps, deep cleaning. The flat looks new; nothing is rebuilt.
  • Mid-scope: bathrooms fully renovated (₹1.0–2.2 lakh each at standard grade), kitchen updated or re-done (a 10–12 running-foot BWR + laminate modular kitchen is ₹95,000–1.4 lakh in cabinetry), flooring overlaid in main areas, full paint, false ceiling in living room.
  • Full gut: everything out — new concealed plumbing and wiring, full retiling and flooring, new kitchen, new bathrooms, new doors/windows where needed. The only original things left are the walls and the view.

The Society Layer: NOC, Deposit, Hours, Debris

  • NOC in writing, first. Before demolition day, not after the society office calls. Standard asks: scope description, contractor and worker details (some societies require police verification), and an undertaking on structural non-interference.
  • Refundable deposit: ₹10,000–1 lakh depending on society and scope — returned after a post-work inspection for common-area damage.
  • Working hours: commonly 9–10 am to 6–7 pm, no Sundays or festival days. Drilling-hour windows can be narrower. Your contractor's schedule must be built on the society's clock, not his.
  • Debris: bagged, moved on the society's schedule, never down the common chute. ₹4,000–8,000 per truckload; a full 2BHK gut job generates 3–8 truckloads. Confirm who pays for lift protection and common-area cleaning — in writing.
  • Redevelopment horizon. Before spending ₹15–25 lakh on a gut renovation, check where your society stands on redevelopment. A building 3–5 years from a redevelopment agreement makes a full renovation money burnt — a refresh-tier job bridges the gap instead. Our society redevelopment guide covers how to read the signals.

Old buildings are a different project

Mumbai has a huge stock of pre-1990 buildings — cess/pagdi buildings, old MHADA colonies, and early co-op societies. Renovating in these adds real line items: surface-to-concealed wiring conversion (old walls resist chasing), shared plumbing stacks that limit how far bathrooms can move, mandatory extra waterproofing on external walls that have weathered 40+ monsoons, and structural caution — some old buildings are load-bearing, where removing any wall is a structural act. Budget 10–20% above the tables for buildings older than ~35 years, and involve a structural engineer before touching any wall you did not build.

Monsoon Rules for Renovation

Indoor trades run year-round; the wet trades do not. In Mumbai specifically, societies often bar debris movement during heavy rain weeks, and external-wall work (grills, windows, ledge waterproofing) is impossible mid-monsoon. Two hard rules: never tile or waterproof over a damp substrate, and if the renovation was triggered by seepage, fix the source first and let the wall dry 2–4 weeks — the full sequence is in our seepage repair cost guide. The efficient calendar: design, NOC and material selection during the rains; demolition in October; handover before the year ends.

Renovation Quote Red Flags

  • One lump sum, no room-wise breakup. You cannot compare or negotiate what you cannot see.
  • No waterproofing line in bathroom scope. Skipping it saves ₹3,000–6,000 and produces seepage to the flat below within 2–3 years — which you will pay to fix.
  • “Society NOC not needed, we know the watchman.” The stop-work notice arrives mid-demolition, with your deposit and schedule as casualties.
  • Structural changes offered casually. Anyone willing to “just remove” a wall without an engineer's opinion should not be inside your building.
  • No debris and cleaning ownership. The commonest end-of-project dispute; settle it in the contract.

Bottom Line — Mumbai Flat Renovation 2026

  • Fix the scope tier first: refresh, mid, or gut — quotes are only comparable within a tier. 2BHK mid-scope: ₹9–16 L; full gut: ₹14–24 L.
  • 1BHKs cost more per sqft than 2BHKs — kitchens and bathrooms don't shrink with the flat.
  • Society NOC in writing before demolition day; budget the deposit (₹10,000–1 lakh) and the society's working-hours calendar.
  • Old buildings (+10–20%) and premium zones change the number more than material choices do.
  • Design and NOC in monsoon, demolition in October, done by December.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does it cost to renovate a 2BHK flat in Mumbai in 2026?+

Depends on scope, not just size. In Mumbai 2026: a refresh (full repaint, minor repairs, fixture swaps) runs ₹2.5–4.5 L; a mid-scope renovation (both bathrooms redone, kitchen updated, full paint, some false ceiling) runs ₹9–16 L; a full gut renovation (all-new plumbing and wiring, full retiling, new kitchen, everything replaced) runs ₹14–24 L. These use the same rate bands as our bathroom (₹1.0–2.2 lakh standard per bathroom), painting and false-ceiling guides.

What does a 1BHK renovation cost in Mumbai?+

For a 400–550 sqft carpet 1BHK in Mumbai: refresh ₹1.5–3 L, mid-scope ₹5–9 L, full gut ₹8–14 L. A 1BHK is not simply half a 2BHK — the kitchen and bathroom are the expensive rooms and a 1BHK has almost as much of them as a 2BHK, which is why per-sqft renovation rates rise as flats get smaller.

Do I need society permission to renovate my flat?+

Yes — get the society NOC in writing before any work starts. Typical conditions: a refundable deposit (₹10,000–1 lakh depending on society and scope), registered contractor and worker details, permitted working hours (commonly 9–10 am to 6–7 pm, no Sundays/holidays), debris rules, and lift-usage restrictions for materials. Purely internal, non-structural renovation generally needs only the society NOC; anything touching structure, external walls, or flat layout/merging needs municipal approval — confirm with an architect before committing.

What can I never touch in a flat renovation?+

Columns, beams, and any load-bearing element — cutting or chasing them is a structural act that endangers the whole building and violates every society bye-law. Also treat with caution: moving bathrooms or kitchens off their plumbing stacks (sunken-slab and stack positions limit how far wet areas can move), and deep slab chipping for flooring (overlay where possible). When in doubt, a structural engineer's one-visit opinion costs a few thousand rupees; a wrong cut costs the building.

Can I renovate during the monsoon?+

Indoor work, yes — carpentry, painting of internal walls (if walls are dry), electrical and modular installation all proceed. What waits: external-wall work, ledge/window waterproofing, and anything needing dry cure times. In Mumbai specifically, societies often bar debris movement during heavy rain weeks, and external-wall work (grills, windows, ledge waterproofing) is impossible mid-monsoon. If your renovation follows a seepage repair, the wall must dry 2–4 weeks after the source fix before putty and paint — see our seepage repair guide.

Is renovation costlier in South Mumbai than the suburbs?+

Yes — expect 25–35% above Borivali/Mulund/Thane rates for identical scope in South Mumbai, Bandra and Juhu. Heritage-precinct rules, zero parking, longer material journeys and shorter permitted working hours all bill to you. Navi Mumbai runs 10–15% below the island-city baseline.
