One stain, very different bills

A damp patch on the wall or ceiling has one appearance and five possible causes — and the repair bill ranges from ₹3,500 for a plumbing joint to ₹2 lakh+ for a terrace re-waterproofing, depending on which one it is. Most money wasted on seepage in India is wasted the same way: treating the stain (repainting, “damp-proof” paint, a coat of cement slurry inside) instead of the source.

This guide does it in the right order: identify the source from the pattern of the damp, then price the correct fix at current 2026 rates, then price the cosmetic repair that comes after the wall has dried.

Step 1 — Read the Damp: 5 Causes and How to Tell Them Apart

Get this step right before calling anyone. A contractor who quotes a fix without first identifying the source is quoting to treat the stain — and the stain always comes back.

The most common overcharge in seepage work

Quoting a full tile-breaking bathroom redo (₹35,000–80,000) when joint injection (₹8,000–25,000) would have worked. Failed tile joints and the floor-wall junction cause the majority of bathroom-origin seepage, and both are fixable by injection without demolition. A fair contractor tests the cheap fix first and puts a re-visit clause in writing; one who opens with “tiles will have to go” before any joint test deserves a second opinion.

Step 3 — The Repaint After the Repair (Don't Skip the Waiting)

The cosmetic repair is the easy part, and the part everyone rushes. The wall must dry for 2–4 weeks after the source is fixed — longer for thick external walls at the end of monsoon — before any putty touches it. Painting over residual moisture is how a ₹12,000 repaint peels in two months.

  • Scrape and prepare: remove all loose paint, flaked putty, and salt deposits back to sound plaster.
  • Putty: ₹10–16/sqft (Birla White / JK White class) on the affected area.
  • Primer + 2-coat emulsion: ₹12–20/sqft for standard acrylic emulsion.
  • All-in patch repaint: budget ₹24–38/sqft of affected wall area. A typical single-wall patch job lands at ₹3,000–9,000; a badly damaged room (ceiling + walls + electrical points) runs ₹15,000–60,000.

What Delay Costs: The Damage Clock

Seepage bills grow non-linearly with time. In the first monsoon, you have a stain. By the second, putty and paint are peeling and the repaint bill has arrived. By the third, water has usually found the electrical conduits (junction boxes in a damp wall are a genuine shock hazard), corroded slab reinforcement begins spalling concrete off the ceiling, and the “cosmetic issue” is structural repair. The interior damage bill alone for a room left leaking across seasons runs ₹15,000–60,000 — frequently more than the source fix would have cost in year one.

Monsoon Triage: What to Do in July vs What Waits for October

  • Do now: diagnose — active seepage is the best diagnostic tool you will ever get; photograph and date every patch. Fix indoor sources immediately: plumbing repairs and bathroom joint injection are all-weather work. Move electricals and furniture away from wet walls. Clear terrace drain mouths.
  • Wait for October–February: exterior wall coatings, terrace waterproofing, and the final repaint — all need dry surfaces and dry curing windows. Doing them mid-monsoon means paying for them twice.
  • Never do: repaint over an active source. It hides the evidence, wastes the money, and the patch returns with interest.

Who Pays in a Flat: You, the Society, or the Neighbour Upstairs?

The principle followed under co-operative society bye-laws (including the model bye-laws adopted by most Maharashtra societies) is source-based: leakage originating inside a member's flat — bathroom waterproofing, internal plumbing — is that owner's repair to fund, even though the damage appears in the ceiling below; leakage from structure and common areas — terrace, external walls, common plumbing stacks — is the society's, paid from its repair fund. The practical sequence: document with dated photos, write to the society (email, not verbal), request a joint inspection to establish the source, and only then discuss who funds what. Adopted bye-laws vary, so check your society's registered copy — and if the upstairs neighbour stonewalls, the society has the authority to direct the repair.

Bottom Line — Seepage Repair 2026

  • Diagnose before you spend. Five sources, one identical stain — and a 50× spread in repair cost between a pipe joint and a terrace.
  • Bathroom-origin leaks: insist on injection first (₹8,000–25,000) before agreeing to tile-breaking (₹35,000–80,000+).
  • Paint is not waterproofing. Source fix → 2–4 weeks drying → putty and repaint at ₹24–38/sqft. In that order only.
  • Monsoon is for diagnosis and indoor fixes; exterior coatings and terrace work wait for the October–February dry window.
  • In flats, the payer follows the source: inside a flat → that owner; terrace and structure → the society. Document everything in writing.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does wall seepage repair cost in India 2026?+

It depends on the source: bathroom leak injection without tile-breaking ₹8,000–25,000; crack injection ₹35–70/sqft of crack area; exterior waterproof coating ₹18–32/sqft (weathercoat) to ₹40–80/sqft (elastomeric); rising damp injection DPC ₹120–250 per running foot; concealed plumbing repair ₹3,500–15,000 all-in. Add ₹24–38/sqft for the repaint after the wall dries.

Can a bathroom leak be fixed without breaking tiles?+

Often yes. Failed tile joints and the floor-wall junction — the most common causes — are fixed by PU/epoxy pressure-injection through small drilled ports plus re-grouting, for ₹8,000–25,000 per bathroom. Tile-breaking (₹35,000–80,000+) is only unavoidable when the concealed membrane has failed across the floor or a concealed pipe is leaking. Test the injection route first.

What does ceiling leakage repair cost?+

Price the source plus the ceiling. Bathroom above: ₹8,000–25,000 (injection) to ₹35,000–80,000 (full redo) — usually the upstairs owner's cost in a flat. Terrace above: waterproofing at ₹140–260/sqft, usually a society expense. The ceiling's own repair (scrape, putty, primer, repaint) adds ₹24–38/sqft, or ₹15,000–60,000 for a badly damaged room, done only after 2–4 weeks of drying.

Can seepage be repaired during the monsoon?+

Indoor fixes, yes — plumbing repairs and bathroom joint injection are all-weather work, and monsoon is actually the best time to diagnose because active seepage shows the water path. Exterior coatings, terrace waterproofing and repainting need dry surfaces, so they wait for October–February. Never repaint a wall with an active source.

Who pays for seepage repair — me, the society, or the upstairs flat?+

The source decides. Leakage originating inside a flat (bathroom waterproofing, internal plumbing) is that flat owner's responsibility even when the damage shows downstairs; leakage from the terrace, external walls or common plumbing is the society's, from its repair fund — this is the principle in the model co-operative bye-laws most Maharashtra societies adopt. Document with dated photos, write to the society, and get a joint inspection to fix the source on record. Registered bye-laws vary by society, so check your copy.

Why does paint keep peeling even after repainting a damp wall?+

Because paint is decoration, not waterproofing. With an active moisture source, vapour pushes through and lifts putty and paint from behind — no “damp-proof” paint survives it for long. Fix the source, let the wall dry 2–4 weeks, scrape to sound plaster, then putty (₹10–16/sqft), primer, and two coats of emulsion (₹12–20/sqft). Repainting first is paying twice.
