PMAY 2.0 Explained — Eligibility, Subsidy & Application Process

The Pradhan Mantri Awas Yojana — Urban 2.0 relaunched on 1 September 2024 with tighter income caps, a stricter property ceiling, and a uniform 4% interest subsidy. For qualifying households it's still worth roughly ₹1.5 lakh in NPV. This guide walks through who actually qualifies, the subsidy math, and the exact application path through the Unified Web Portal.

What PMAY 2.0 is — and how it differs from PMAY 1.0

PMAY-U 2.0 is the Interest Subsidy Scheme (ISS) component of the second iteration of the Pradhan Mantri Awas Yojana — Urban mission, approved by the Union Cabinet in August 2024 and operational from 1 September 2024 through 31 August 2029. It targets first-time urban home buyers in lower- and middle-income brackets with an upfront subsidy credited to their home loan account.

If you remember the original PMAY (CLSS — Credit Linked Subsidy Scheme), launched in 2015 and closed for new applications on 31 March 2022 for EWS/LIG (and a year earlier for MIG), the 2.0 version is materially narrower in three ways:

  • Income ceiling dropped sharply. The old MIG-II bracket covered households up to ₹18 lakh annually. The new scheme caps total household income at ₹9 lakh — half the old ceiling.
  • Property cap dropped. Old MIG-II allowed property values up to ₹45 lakh. The new scheme caps it at ₹35 lakh uniformly.
  • Subsidy rate is now uniform. The old scheme had differential rates: 6.5% for EWS/LIG, 4% for MIG-I, 3% for MIG-II. PMAY 2.0 applies a flat 4% subsidy across all categories.
Why does this matter? If you missed PMAY 1.0 because applications closed before you were ready, the 2.0 version is real and available — but only if your household income is genuinely under ₹9 lakh and you're buying a property at or below ₹35 lakh. The middle-class buyer earning ₹12-15 lakh, who would have qualified under MIG-II in 2018, is no longer eligible.

The three income brackets

PMAY-U 2.0 splits eligible households into three income brackets. The subsidy structure and the female ownership rule differ slightly across them.

"Household income" means the combined gross annual income of all earning family members residing in the same dwelling — husband, wife, and any earning children (married children typically counted as separate households if they live elsewhere). The hard cap is ₹9 lakh; ₹9,00,001 disqualifies you.

Six hard eligibility conditions

You must meet ALL six of these to qualify for the ISS subsidy. Failing any one of them disqualifies your application entirely.

  1. Household income up to ₹9 lakh annually. Combined gross income of all earning family members.
  2. No pucca house anywhere in India in any family member's name. "Pucca" means a permanent structure with non-temporary roof and walls. Owning a kutcha hut or a piece of agricultural land does not disqualify.
  3. Not a prior beneficiary of any central, state, or local government housing scheme in the last 20 years. This includes the original PMAY (1.0), state-level housing schemes, slum redevelopment grants, etc.
  4. Property priced at ₹35 lakh or less. This is the all-in registered price including parking and PLC charges. GST is separate. Stamp duty and registration are paid by the buyer separately and don't count toward the ₹35L cap.
  5. Home loan amount of ₹25 lakh or less. The subsidy applies only to the first ₹8 lakh of the loan, but the loan itself cannot exceed ₹25 lakh.
  6. Carpet area up to 120 square metres (approximately 1,292 sqft). This is generous — covers most 2BHK and small 3BHK flats.
The "prior beneficiary" condition trips up more applicants than any other If anyone in your immediate family (parents, siblings still living with you, spouse) received any housing benefit from a government scheme in the last 20 years, the entire household is disqualified — not just that one person. Check carefully: state schemes like AP Housing Board, MHADA lottery, slum redevelopment allotments, and pre-2015 IAY (Indira Awas Yojana) rural housing all count.

How the ₹1.80 lakh subsidy math actually works

The headline number — ₹1.80 lakh per family — is the gross subsidy paid out over 5 equal annual instalments of ₹36,000 each, credited directly to your home loan account by the lender (the PLI — Primary Lending Institution).

The mechanically more important number is the ₹1.50 lakh NPV (Net Present Value), computed at the prescribed 8.5% discount rate. This is what reduces your outstanding principal upfront. The lender then recalculates your EMI on the lower principal at their regular interest rate.

Worked example: ₹15 lakh loan, 20-year tenure, 8% bank rate

EMI before subsidy
Loan amount₹15,00,000
Rate / tenure8.0% / 240 months
Monthly EMI₹12,544
After PMAY 2.0 subsidy applied
Principal reduced by NPV₹15,00,000 − ₹1,50,000 = ₹13,50,000
Rate / tenure unchanged8.0% / 240 months
New EMI₹11,290
Monthly saving₹1,254

Over 20 years, that ₹1,254 monthly saving compounds to roughly ₹3 lakh of total cash flow improvement. The reason the lifetime benefit is larger than the ₹1.80L gross subsidy is that the principal reduction also avoids 20 years of compounded interest on that principal.

Sweet spot for maximum benefit The 4% subsidy applies only to the first ₹8 lakh of your loan. So if you borrow ₹8 lakh exactly, you capture 100% of the subsidy. Borrowing ₹15 lakh you capture the same absolute subsidy but it's a smaller percentage of your loan. The marginal benefit per rupee of loan is highest when your loan is around ₹8-10 lakh — which fits the EWS/LIG segment naturally.

The female ownership requirement

This is one of PMAY 2.0's most consequential design choices and often misunderstood.

For EWS (up to ₹3L income) and LIG (₹3-6L) categories, the property must be registered in the name of a female family member — either as sole owner or jointly with the male earner. The female owner does not need to be the loan applicant; she only needs to be on the registered title deed.

For MIG (₹6-9L), female ownership is not mandatory. The legislature judged the gender-protective measure less critical at this income level.

The policy rationale: research on low-income Indian households consistently shows that female asset ownership reduces domestic violence risk, improves child education outcomes, and stabilises household finances. The 2.0 redesign hard-coded this insight rather than treating it as a soft preference.

Practical workaround for EWS/LIG male-earner households If you fall in EWS/LIG and the only earner in the family is male, register the property jointly with your wife, mother, sister, or daughter. She doesn't need to be a loan co-applicant — she just needs to be on the registered deed. As a bonus, many states (Maharashtra, Karnataka, UP, Haryana, Delhi) also offer 1% stamp duty concession for female buyers, which compounds the benefit.

Documents and the Unified Web Portal flow

PMAY-U 2.0 uses a centralised Unified Web Portal at pmaymis.gov.in. You don't directly apply to MoHUA. The lender (PLI) handles your subsidy claim through the portal within 30 days of loan disbursement.

Documents to keep ready

  • Aadhaar cards of all family members — mandatory for DBT (Direct Benefit Transfer) linkage to the loan account.
  • PAN cards of all earning family members.
  • Income proof. Salaried: Form 16 + 3 months' salary slips. Self-employed: 2 years of ITR.
  • Bank statement (last 6 months).
  • Address proof — any of Voter ID, Aadhaar, utility bill, ration card.
  • Property documents — sale agreement or builder allotment letter.
  • Self-declaration of non-ownership. A signed statement that you do not own a pucca house anywhere in India and have not previously been a PMAY beneficiary.
  • Income certificate. For EWS/LIG, a Tehsildar-issued income certificate is preferred but self-attestation is accepted for incomes up to ₹9L.

The application sequence

  1. Identify a PLI — most major Indian banks (SBI, PNB, HDFC, ICICI, BoB, Axis, IDBI, Kotak), housing finance companies (HDFC Ltd legacy book, LIC HFC, PNB HFL), and many regional cooperative banks are empanelled. Confirm "PMAY 2.0 ISS empanelled" before applying.
  2. Apply for home loan as usual. Submit your documents and the self-declaration. Indicate that you wish to claim PMAY 2.0 ISS during application.
  3. Lender does eligibility validation via the Unified Web Portal. The portal cross-checks against PMAY 1.0 records, IT department data (income), and Aadhaar (identity + family).
  4. Loan sanction and disbursement proceed normally. The subsidy is NOT pre-disbursement; it comes after the loan is disbursed.
  5. Lender submits subsidy claim within 30 days of disbursement. MoHUA processes the claim through the National Housing Bank (or NHB-equivalent administering body).
  6. Subsidy credited to loan account. The principal is reduced by the NPV (~₹1.5L) upfront, and your EMI is recalculated on the lower principal. Your lender notifies you of the revised schedule.

Five mistakes that disqualify otherwise eligible buyers

  1. Mis-stating household income. The portal cross-checks against IT department records. Understating income to qualify is detected and disqualifies the application — sometimes with future-scheme consequences.
  2. Forgetting a family member's prior housing benefit. If a parent received an IAY (rural housing) grant in 2010, the family is technically disqualified for 20 years. Check Aadhaar-linked benefit records before applying.
  3. Buying a property above ₹35L "with parking/PLC adjustments." The cap is the all-in registered consideration. Asking the builder to lower the registered price below ₹35L while paying the difference in cash is criminal evasion and gets caught in CERSAI/IGRS validations.
  4. EWS/LIG buyer registering property in male earner's name alone. Once registered, the disqualification is irreversible without re-registration (which costs another full stamp duty). Get this right at first registration.
  5. Applying after loan disbursement. The subsidy claim must be filed within 30 days of disbursement. Late claims are rejected. If your lender is unfamiliar with PMAY 2.0, push them or switch PLIs before signing.
