Tile-on-Tile: The ₹12–25/sqft Shortcut, With Rules

Overlaying new tiles on old skips removal and debris entirely — legitimate, common, and safe if: the old floor passes a full tap-test (hollow tiles come up first), you use polymer tile-on-tile adhesive (never a cement bed on a glazed surface), and the household accepts a 15–20mm floor rise — doors trimmed, thresholds stepped. Where not to do it: bathrooms. The rise fights the drainage slope, and an old bathroom floor frequently hides the seepage you are about to tile over — the sequence for that problem is in our seepage repair guide.

Bed vs Adhesive (When Each Is Right)

  • Cement-sand bed (40–50mm): fine for standard floor tiles up to 600x600 on bare slab; time-tested; adds significant floor height.
  • Polymer adhesive (3–6mm): mandatory for tile-on-tile, large formats, anything above 800x800, and all wall dado; the only option when matching existing floor levels in renovations.
  • The hollow-tile test: whichever method, tap every tile after curing — hollow sound means voids under the tile, and voids mean cracks within a year. Make the tap-test a handover condition in writing.

Quote Red Flags

  • “Per sqft” without saying what's inside. Labour-only vs with-adhesive vs with-bed differ by 40%+. Itemise.
  • Removal “as actual”. Fix the ₹/sqft and the debris cartage owner before demolition, not after.
  • Large format at small-format rates. The mason who agrees to lay 1200x1200 at ₹30/sqft is the mason who gives you lippage.
  • No wastage math. 5–8% wastage standard sizes, 10–15% large formats and diagonal layouts — material orders should show it.
  • Skirting and beading unpriced. Small lines, standard end-of-job dispute.

Bottom Line — Tiling in 2026

  • Standard vitrified: labour ₹25–40/sqft, all-in redo ₹100–170/sqft. Large format: labour ₹60–120, all-in ₹190–420.
  • Tile-on-tile saves removal money but is banned in bathrooms and conditional everywhere else.
  • Adhesive is mandatory above 800x800, on walls, and over old tiles; beds are for standard tiles on bare slab.
  • Tap-test at handover, in the contract.
  • Compare quotes only after forcing them into the same unit: labour + adhesive + removal, itemised.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the tile laying labour cost per sqft in India 2026?+

Laying labour in 2026: standard 600x600 vitrified tiles ₹25–40/sqft, 800x800 ₹30–50/sqft, large-format planks and slabs (1200x600 and up) ₹60–120/sqft, Indian marble ₹35–55/sqft, Italian marble ₹45–70/sqft. Wall dado (kitchens, bathrooms) runs ₹35–60/sqft because vertical work is slower. Add adhesive + grout at ₹8–18/sqft, or a conventional cement-sand bed at roughly comparable cost for standard floor tiles.

What does a complete flooring redo cost per sqft?+

All-in for replacing existing flooring with standard vitrified: old floor removal ₹12–25/sqft + laying labour ₹25–40 + adhesive/bed and grout ₹8–18 + tile material ₹45–90 (standard vitrified) = roughly ₹100–170/sqft, or ₹120–220/sqft with better tiles and large formats. A 700 sqft carpet 2BHK's main areas (500–550 sqft excluding bathrooms) therefore run ₹55,000–1.2 lakh — the flooring share our flat renovation guides allocate.

Can I lay new tiles over old tiles (tile-on-tile)?+

Often yes, and it saves the ₹12–25/sqft removal plus debris cost. Conditions: the old floor must be sound (no hollow-sounding tiles — tap-test the whole floor), you must use polymer tile adhesive (₹15–35/sqft for tile-on-tile grade), not a cement bed, and you must accept a ~15–20mm floor height rise — doors need trimming and thresholds change. Avoid tile-on-tile in bathrooms: the height rise fights the drainage slope, and old bathroom floors often hide the leak you are about to seal in.

Why do large-format tiles cost so much more to lay?+

Three reasons: handling (a 1200x1200 tile needs two workers and suction lifters), substrate (large formats demand a flatter floor — self-levelling or laser-screed preparation is often a separate ₹20–40/sqft line), and skill (lippage — edge misalignment — is glaring on big tiles, so you are paying for the better mason). Add higher wastage on cuts. Budget ₹60–120/sqft labour plus mandatory adhesive, against ₹25–40 for standard sizes.

What are skirting and grouting charged at?+

Skirting: ₹15–30 per running foot fitted, using strips cut from your floor tiles (add the tile material consumed). Grouting is normally inside the laying rate with cement grout; epoxy grout — stain-proof, the right answer for kitchen dado and bathroom floors — is an upgrade at ₹8–15/sqft. Spacers, corner beading for dado edges (₹20–40/rft), and threshold strips are small lines worth itemising so they don't appear as surprises.

Should tiles be laid on a cement bed or with adhesive?+

Standard 600x600 floor tiles: either works — the conventional 40–50mm cement-sand bed is time-tested and similar in cost. Mandatory adhesive cases: tile-on-tile, large formats, wall dado above 4 ft, and any tile over 800x800 (bed-laying big tiles causes hollow spots and cracks). Adhesive also adds only 3–6mm of height vs the bed's 40–50mm — decisive when matching existing floor levels in a renovation.
