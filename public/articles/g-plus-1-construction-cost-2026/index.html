Nobody plans a house in per-sqft rates. You plan it as “a G+1 on my 30x40 plot” — and then discover that every builder's blog quotes a different number for the same thing, usually for one city and always in a way that ends in a sales call. This guide does the arithmetic neutrally: 2026 rates by plot size and floor count, the setback math that converts plot area into buildable area, and the two economic facts about floors that most quotes never explain.

The two facts first

1) Your first floor is the cheapest floor you will ever build — roughly 8–12% less per sqft than the ground floor, because the foundation, excavation and plinth are already paid for. 2) Whether you can build it depends on decisions made at foundation stage. Designing footings for a future floor costs 3–6% of the structure package today; retrofitting capacity later costs ₹8,000–18,000 per column, if it is possible at all.

The Assumptions (So You Can Adjust Them)

  • Rates: the same bands as our Construction Cost Calculator — economy ₹850–1,400/sqft, standard ₹1,150–1,900/sqft, premium ₹1,700–2,800/sqft, where the low end is Tier-3 towns and the high end is metros. All figures below use the standard band.
  • Floor plate: ~80% of plot area per floor after typical setbacks. A 30x40 (1,200 sqft) plot yields roughly a 950 sqft floor plate; local bylaws vary, so treat ±5–10% as normal.
  • Scope: civil construction only — structure, plaster, basic flooring, standard electrical and plumbing. Land, interiors, compound wall, borewell, approvals and external development are extra.

Why G+1 Is the Sweet Spot: The Floor Economics

Every floor of a house does not cost the same. Here is where the money actually sits:

  • Ground floor carries the fixed costs. Excavation, foundation, plinth beam, ground-floor slab, water and sewage connections — typically 15–20% of a single-floor project is below your ankles. Build only a ground floor, and every one of those rupees is amortised over one floor.
  • The first floor rides for less. Columns up, slab, walls, finishes — roughly 8–12% cheaper per sqft than the ground floor because it shares the foundation. This is the core arithmetic behind duplexes and rental-unit builds.
  • But floors are not free of overheads. Each level adds a staircase flight (60–80 sqft of plate area per floor, ₹1–2.5 lakh of construction), material-lifting labour, sanitary stacks and, at G+2 and beyond, heavier structural members that start clawing back the per-sqft saving.
  • Net result: a G+1 costs slightly less than 2× the ground floor alone, and G+2 slightly less than 3× — until height-driven structure costs and (in some cities) lift requirements flip the curve around G+3/G+4.

FAR, Setbacks and Road Width: What Actually Limits Your Floors

Three rules decide the ceiling on your floor count, and none of them is the strength of your columns:

  • FAR/FSI (Floor Area Ratio): total built-up area ÷ plot area. Typical residential FAR of 1.5–2.0 allows roughly G+1 to G+3 on standard plots once setbacks are counted.
  • Setbacks: mandatory open strips on all sides that shrink the floor plate to ~75–85% of plot area — already baked into our tables.
  • Height and road width: many cities cap residential height (commonly 10–15 m) and tie permissible floors to the width of the abutting road. A narrow lane can cap you at G+1 even when FAR allows G+3.

Build exactly what the sanctioned plan shows. Unsanctioned floors risk penalties, refusal of the occupancy certificate, and denial of permanent water and electricity connections — a discount that costs more than it saves.

Building G Now, Adding a Floor Later? Spend the 3–6% Today

If there is any chance of a future floor, tell your structural engineer before the foundation drawing is issued. Designing footings and columns for one or two extra floors adds about 3–6% to the structure package — roughly 1–2.5% of total project cost. The alternative, discovered by thousands of families each year, is retrofitting: column jacketing at ₹8,000–18,000 per column after a structural assessment, or an engineer's report saying the addition is not feasible at all. We cover that whole scenario — feasibility, permissions, and full costing — in the companion guide: Cost to Add a Floor on an Existing House.

Bottom Line — G+1 in 2026

  • Standard-quality G+1: ₹11–18 L on 20x30, ₹22–36 L on 30x40, ₹28–46 L on 30x50, ₹44–72 L on 40x60 — bottom of band for Tier-3, top for metros. Civil construction only.
  • The first floor is 8–12% cheaper per sqft than the ground floor; G+1 is the sweet spot before staircases, stacks and structure claw the saving back.
  • Duplex vs rental-unit configuration changes your finances more than any material choice — decide before the design is frozen.
  • FAR, setbacks and road width cap your floors, not your columns. Build to the sanctioned plan.
  • Future floor even remotely possible? Pay the 3–6% structure premium for a stronger foundation now.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the G+1 construction cost for a 30x40 site in 2026?+

About 1,900 sqft of built-up area after setbacks: ₹16–21 lakh economy, ₹22–36 lakh standard, ₹32–53 lakh premium. Metros price at the top of each band, Tier-2/3 at the bottom. Land, interiors, approvals and external works are extra.

Is the first floor cheaper to build than the ground floor?+

Yes, by roughly 8–12% per sqft — the foundation, excavation and plinth are already paid for. Some of the saving goes back into the staircase (60–80 sqft per floor plus ₹1–2.5 lakh), lifting labour and the overhead tank, which is why a G+1 totals slightly less than 2× a ground-only build.

Can I build a G+1 house in ₹30 lakh?+

Comfortably, on a 30x40 plot at standard quality outside metros (₹22–29 lakh at ₹1,150–1,500/sqft). In a metro at ₹1,900/sqft the same house is ~₹36 lakh, so ₹30 lakh means economy-to-standard specs or a smaller floor plate. Interiors add ₹8–15 lakh more for a full fit-out.

How many floors can I build on my plot?+

FAR/FSI, height caps and abutting road width decide — typically G+1 to G+3 on standard residential plots at FAR 1.5–2.0. A narrow road can cap you below what FAR allows. Confirm with the sanctioned plan; unsanctioned floors risk penalties and denial of occupancy certificate and utilities.

Should I design the foundation for extra floors now?+

If a future floor is even remotely possible, yes. It adds ~3–6% to the structure package today (about 1–2.5% of project cost). Retrofitting later means column jacketing at ₹8,000–18,000 per column — or an engineer telling you the addition is not feasible.

How long does a G+1 take to build?+

8–12 months end to end: 1–2 months design and sanction, 4–6 months structure (each extra floor adds ~6–10 weeks of slab cycles), 3–4 months finishing. Casting roof slabs before June avoids the standard monsoon delay.
